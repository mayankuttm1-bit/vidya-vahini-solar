The Soiling Loss Fact

In Eastern Uttar Pradesh, high atmospheric particulate matter (PM2.5 / PM10) from crop residue stubble burning, dry alluvial loam dust, and industrial transport in areas like Naini and Phaphamau forms an invisible microscopic barrier on glass surfaces. An uncleaned 5 kW solar plant loses approximately 3.5 to 5 units of electricity every single day, costing owners over ₹1,200 per month in wasted generation.

1. The Physics of Soiling: Why Rain Isn't Enough

A frequent misconception among rooftop owners is believing occasional rainfall will keep panels clean. While heavy monsoon rains do wash away loose topsoil, light showers or winter mist actually worsen soiling by turning airborne dust into a thick, baked-on cement-like crust.

This causes two primary engineering hazards:

  • Optical Transmission Loss: Dirt particles absorb and scatter solar photons before they reach the silicon semiconductor PN junction, causing an immediate drop in short-circuit current (Isc).
  • Localized "Hotspot" Formation: Bird droppings, stubborn tree sap, or leaf debris create concentrated shading over a single solar cell. That shaded cell stops generating power and turns into an electrical resistance load, dissipating excess current as intense localized heat (often exceeding 130°C), which permanently delaminates solar EVA sheets.

2. The 5 Golden Rules of Cleaning Solar Panels

Improper cleaning can cause more financial damage than dirty panels. Follow these strict EPC guidelines:

1 Never Clean Panels Under Hot Midday Sun (Thermal Shock Hazard)

When solar modules sit in 45°C summer heat in Prayagraj, the glass surface temperature reaches 70°C. Spraying cold tap water (22°C) onto hot glass induces immediate thermal shock, creating micro-fractures in the tempered glass or shattering it completely. Always clean before 8:00 AM or after 6:00 PM.

2 Check Water Quality (Avoid Hard Borewell Water)

Groundwater across parts of Prayagraj has high total dissolved solids (TDS > 500 ppm) with heavy calcium and magnesium carbonates. Washing panels with hard water leaves white chalky mineral scale deposits as water evaporates. Use soft water, rainwater, or demineralized (DM) water whenever possible.

3 Use Soft Microfiber Mops — Strictly No Abrasives

Modern Tier-1 panels (such as Loom Solar TopCon bifacial modules) feature nanoscale Anti-Reflective Coatings (ARC) on both surfaces. Using wire brushes, abrasive sponges, or broom bristles scratches this coating, permanently lowering light transmission. Use soft microfiber squeegees.

4 Never Walk or Step on Solar Modules

Even though tempered solar glass can withstand hailstones, human body weight concentrates severe pressure on silicon wafers underneath the glass. While the outer glass may not shatter, silicon cells develop microscopic hairline cracks that degrade output over subsequent years.

5 Avoid Harsh Chemicals or Acidic Cleaners

Detergents, floor cleaners, or caustic chemicals corrode the anodized aluminum module frames and compromise silicone waterproofing seals around the junction box. Use clean water or specialized pH-neutral solar cleaning solutions.

3. Recommended Cleaning Schedule in Eastern UP

Season / Months Environmental Conditions Cleaning Interval Action Protocol
Winter (Nov – Feb) Heavy fog, stagnant smog & agricultural dust Every 10 to 14 Days Early morning wash with microfiber mop
Summer (Mar – Jun) High winds, loose topsoil, harvest dust Every 12 to 15 Days Clean before 7:30 AM before panels heat up

4. Beyond the Glass: Electrical Balance of Plant Checklist

A solar power plant is an active electrical substation operating at up to 800V DC. Maintenance must include the electrical infrastructure:

Inverter Care

Heat Sink & Fan Check

Check that inverter cooling fins are clear of cobwebs and wasp nests. High ambient heat causes inverters to thermal-throttle and reduce generation.

Earthing Pits

Soil Moisture & Ohms

Pour water into chemical earthing chambers twice during hot summer months to keep grounding resistance safely below 2 Ohms.

Structural Hardware

Torque Inspection

Inspect SS-304 fastener bolts and HDG foundation anchors annually to ensure wind gusts haven't caused mechanical vibration looseness.
